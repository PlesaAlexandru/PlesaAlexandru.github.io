## Scenario

As a member of the IT department at SwiftSpend Financial, you are responsible for assisting employees with technical concerns. What initially appeared to be a routine day quickly escalated when multiple employees across different departments reported receiving a suspicious email. Several users noted unusual characteristics in the message, and unfortunately, some had already submitted their credentials and were no longer able to access their accounts. With the potential for a wider compromise, the incident has been escalated for investigation. Your task is to analyze the available evidence, determine the scope of the attack, and uncover how the adversary operated.

---
## Objective

- Analyze the provided email samples to identify key artifacts
- Investigate phishing URLs to understand redirection
- Retrieve and examine the phishing kit used in the attack
- Use CTI tools to gather intelligence on the adversary
- Analyze the phishing kit to uncover additional indicators

---
## Summary

**Description**: We received 5 phishing mails to 5 different employees on `29/06/2020 06:01` (dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm). All mails had malicious intent via credential gathering while pretending to be a legitimate login page (Microsoft). After further investigation we could see that the employees interacted with the attack and credentials were stolen. Based on the investigation, remediation must be done immediately.

#### Overview Investigation

At first, we will analyse the mail overview before checking for technical details such as: SPF, DKIM, DMARC.
Let's analyse the Sender first. 
`https[:]//groupmarketingonline[.]icu` is not an reachable site. Which is already an strong indicator of an phishing attempt but due to the old email date it could also be an legitimate service which ended some time ago. Also, `.icu` is not an high-reputation TLD.

![](../../assets/Pasted%20image%2020261103220138.png)

Next, we can check for the body content and subject at matter.
First thing first, typically phishing emails contain a lot of grammatical errors, in our case:

| **Wrong** / Unproffesional          | Right / Proffesional                                              |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wrong**: `a Direct Credit Advice` | **Right**: `a direct credit advice notice / the direct credit advice` |
| **Wrong**: `Direct Credit Advices`  | **Right**: `direct credit advice notices / direct credit advice`      |
| **Wrong**: `hardcopy`               | **Right**: `hard copy`                                                |
| **Wrong**: `or phoning us.`         | **Right**: `or by calling us. / or by phone.`                         |
| **Wrong**: `Good Day!`              | **Right**: `Good day, / Dear Customer,`                               |
Second, to verify the legitimacy of the service we can check the links from their social media pages.

![](../../assets/Pasted%20image%2020261027140547.png)
![](../../assets/Pasted%20image%2020261027140600.png)
![](../../assets/Pasted%20image%2020261027140609.png)

The only interesting detail is that they have an `Pinterest` page. Which is kind off unusual for an service that provides monetary advices for banks and financial services.

Right under the pages, we can spot an fake HQ address:
`31337 Waysbroad, New Island`.

Generic phone numbers:
`(800) 555-0299`, `(800) 555-0199`

Let's have a look at the attachment, we download and get the hash into an isolated environment to further investigate and note the IOC.

The low file size of the HTML file could a red flag due to the possibility of being an redirect source to something else or simulated login page to steal data.

By accessing in an sandbox we get the following result: 

**URL:** `http[:]//kennaroads[.]buzz/data/Update365/office365/40e7baa2f826a57fcf04e5202526f8bd/enterpassword.php?b43GLA17931013731bcfe686da02fad7eb15254e3a26b10e1bcfe686da02fad7eb15254e3a26b10e1bcfe686da02fad7eb15254e3a26b10e1bcfe686da02fad7eb15254e3a26b10e1bcfe686da02fad7eb15254e3a26b10e&email=derick.marshall[at]swiftspend[.]finance&error=`

The "advice" is actually an impersonation of an legitimate service (Microsoft). The URL doesn't corresponds with the service at all, instead of `microsoft[.]com` for example, we see `kennaroads[.]buzz`.
It is a page that collects data such as email & password combination and maybe IPs.
So, even if the file itself is not malicious, it is malicious intended.

## Scenario:

A sales executive at Greenholt PLC has reported a suspicious email received from a known customer. The message raised several red flags: a generic greeting, an unexpected request for a money transfer, and an unsolicited attachment. According to the employee, this behavior does not align with the customer’s usual communication style. Concerned that the email may be malicious, the message has been escalated to the SOC (Security Operations Center) for further investigation. Your goal is to analyze the provided email sample and determine whether it is legitimate or part of a phishing attempt.

## Objective

- Analyze the provided email to identify and extract key artifacts
- Investigate the message source to determine its origin and authenticity
- Use analysis tools to assess the potential maliciousness of the email

## Investigation


Before checking the SPF, DKIM, DMARC. We are going to analyze the mail received to find any IOCs that could instantly indicate the maliciousness of the email.

Different IOCs and Malicious behaviour was spotted in this email at first sight.
- Not referring to the person in context but to it's email address (`Good day webmaster[at]redacted[.]org`)
- Unprofessional spacing, formatting and design (Unusual for a professional bank)
- Typos (`funds has been transferred` instead of `funds have been transferred`)
- Hiding real file extension from attachment. (`____PDF__.CAB` instead of `.pdf` for an legitimate PDF file or `.CAB` for an cabinet file extension like in our case)
- Soft hiding Urgency via colour (Bright red to mark importance) and fake transaction receipt on huge amounts (Phishing attempt via transactions are an easy way due to the curiousity factor, an user would be more interested in the Transfer status being successful than the email subtle details).
- No support account mentioned in cases of issues.
- Bank charges and amount not lining up (Formatting `xxx.xxx` and then `xxx,xx` the cents are mentioned in one place but not in the other)
- Format not corresponding to SWIFT MT103.

Let's talk about important details missing from our Phishing attempt.
- **UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference):** This is the single most important detail. It is a 36-character string (formatted like `xxxxxxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx`). It allows banks to track the payment in real-time. **Without this, you cannot track a delayed payment.**
- **Sender's Reference (Field 20):** A unique reference number assigned by the sending bank to identify the operation.
- **Charge Details (Field 71A):** This specifies who pays the transfer fees. It must say either **OUR** (sender pays all), **SHA** (shared fees), or **BEN** (beneficiary pays all).
- **Sender (Field 50A/K):** The ordering customer's full name, account number/IBAN, and address.

- **Loki / LokiBot / LokiLocker:** The specific malware family. Depending on the variant, it primarily functions as an **infostealer** or a **ransomware**.
